# Historical case study: En Marche's 2016 Grande Marche and the 2017 election

This documentation supplies source provenance for `archive/campaign.html`. The animation concerns historical campaign **operations and aggregate public election results**. It does not seek to prescribe, target or optimize persuasive political communication.

## What was in the original ESCP study

The uploaded 94-page team report, *Big Data : comment l'empêcher de devenir un cheval de Troie*, discusses digital political campaigning, cites En Marche and the Parti socialiste, and questions whether political Big Data is effective. It does not document a complete causal account of the 2017 presidential election. The new website case study is explicitly a 2026 externally sourced expansion, not a reconstruction of unrecorded claims by the coauthors.

## Sources and attribution

- **Official first and second round election results:** French Ministry of the Interior, [2017 definitive archive](https://www.archives-resultats-elections.interieur.gouv.fr/resultats/presidentielle-2017/FE.php). Percentages refer to *suffrages exprimés* (valid ballots); voters, blanks, and turnout are separate denominators. Macron 24.01% in round one; 66.10% in round two.
- **Campaign organization and field canvassing:** [Le Parisien, May 21, 2016](https://www.leparisien.fr/archives/dans-les-coulisses-de-la-grande-marche-de-macron-21-05-2016-5814815.php): reported use of past electoral returns and INSEE aggregate sociodemographic data to organize canvassing, with LMP consultancy.
- **Field operation, interviews, diagnosis:** [Carnegie Endowment, 2018](https://carnegieendowment.org/europe/research/2018/04/en-marche-from-a-movement-to-a-government) reports more than 5,000 volunteers and around 25,000 substantive interviews; [Le Monde, Oct. 7 2016](https://www.lemonde.fr/les-decodeurs/article/2016/10/07/emmanuel-macron-la-grande-marche-et-ses-chiffres-flous_5009682_4355770.html) notes the distinction between the claimed 100,000 conversations and roughly one quarter that number of completed questionnaires, with methodological questions.
- **Academic critical discussion:** [Université Grenoble Alpes journal, 2018](https://lesenjeux.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/2018/dossier/03-une-economie-de-la-promesse-mythes-et-croyances-pour-vendre-du-big-data-electoral/).

## Interpretation rules

The flow graphic is a **conceptual illustration**, not a visualization of private campaign data. No voter microdata, location profiles, or derived persuasion scores are used or displayed. The official election outcomes do not identify causal effects of any campaign strategy. Causal identification would require an appropriate research design, and cannot be inferred from the sequence of events or from these aggregate percentages alone. Readers should distinguish the original team paper's discussion from the later sourced additions.
